TG Media is Tom McTigue  /  Warwick, UK

One person, the whole way through.

I design and build websites for a living, on my own, under the name TG Media. If you work with me you deal with me — the first conversation, the design, the code, and whatever comes up two years later.

Design and code have not been two separate jobs here since the beginning.

  1. Creative direction
  2. UX and content structure
  3. Visual design
  4. Front-end build
  5. CMS implementation

How I work

I like a straightforward brief and a straight conversation. Early on I will ask more questions than you expect, because most website problems turn out to be structure or content problems wearing a visual disguise. Once we agree what the site is for, I would rather show you real pages than mood boards.

I do not have a house style I apply to everyone. A music publisher and a stone worktop business should not end up with the same website wearing different colours, and the fastest way to make a design feel generic is to reuse the last one. What stays consistent is the standard of the work, not the look of it.

Technical decisions get made on what the project actually needs. Sometimes that is a fast custom build; often it is WordPress because your team needs to publish on a Friday afternoon without me. I will tell you which and why, in language that does not require a developer to translate.

When something genuinely needs another pair of hands — photography, film, a specialist integration — I bring in people I have worked with before and stay responsible for the result. What I do not do is quietly hand your project to someone else.

Who the work suits

Businesses with real character and a website that no longer shows it.

Publishers, production studios and creative companies, and equally a salon, a stone specialist or a company that simply makes things properly. The common factor is not the sector. It is that the business has substance and the presentation has fallen behind it.

The work goes best with people who care about what they are building, prefer a direct conversation, and want a creative partner rather than another supplier. You do not need to know what the site should look like — that is my job — but you should know what your business is trying to do. If what you need is the cheapest possible template by Friday, I am the wrong person, and I will say so rather than take the work.
