Clarify

Working out what the site is actually for. Early on I ask more questions than you expect, because most website problems turn out to be structure and content problems wearing a visual disguise. Once we agree what the site has to do, everything after it gets easier and cheaper.

  • Discovery and website strategy
  • Competitor review
  • Digital positioning
  • Content and information architecture
  • Creative direction

02

Brand &
Creative

Position

Giving the business a look that belongs to it rather than one borrowed from its sector. I do not have a house style I apply to everyone. A music publisher and a stone worktop business should not end up with the same website wearing different colours. What stays consistent is the standard, not the look.

  • Brand strategy
  • Visual identity
  • Art direction
  • Brand guidelines
  • Photography and motion direction

03

Designing something people can actually use, on the device they actually have, without sanding off the character that made it worth doing properly. Accessible by default, not as a retrofit.

  • Accessibility
  • User testing where appropriate

Cost

Every quote is written for the project in front of me.

There is no package price, because two businesses asking for “a website” can be a month of work apart. After a proper conversation you get a written proposal: what is included, what it costs, how long it takes, and what would change the number. If something in it is not worth the money, I will say so before you ask.

What moves the price

  • Scope and number of unique templates
  • Condition of the existing content
  • Whether brand work is needed
  • CMS, and who needs to edit what
  • Integrations and third-party systems
  • Amount of motion and custom interaction
  • Migration from an existing site
  • Copywriting and photography support
  • Timescale, and how fixed it is
